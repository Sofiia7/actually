Democrats widen generic-ballot lead as the fight for the House enters the home stretch

With ninety-four days until the vote, both parties are pouring money into a shrinking map of truly competitive districts.
By Adrian Wells · National Politics Desk · August 2, 2026

A fresh wave of national surveys this week showed Democrats stretching their advantage on the generic congressional ballot to the widest margin of the cycle, buoyed by strong summer fundraising and a burst of candidate recruitment in suburban districts both parties once considered safely red.

Strategists in both camps caution that an August polling edge is no guarantee of a majority in November. Redistricting has left fewer than three dozen seats genuinely in play, and much of the challengers’ cash advantage is concentrated in races they are already favored to win.

Still, the map has plainly shifted. A string of retirements in battleground districts has forced the majority to defend open seats it did not budget for, while special-election results since spring have run several points ahead of the fundamentals.

Party committees on both sides say they will treat Labor Day as the true starting gun. Until then, the clearest signal may come not from any single poll, but from where the money goes.
