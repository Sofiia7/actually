Fed officials line up behind a September quarter-point hike as inflation stays sticky

Three regional bank presidents used appearances this week to prepare markets for another increase, arguing services prices are cooling more slowly than the committee projected in June.
By Priya Raman · August 2, 2026, 9:14 a.m. ET

Futures tied to the federal funds rate now assign roughly a three-in-five chance to a 25-basis-point move at the September 15–16 meeting, up from even odds a month ago, while two-year Treasury yields this week touched their highest level since spring.

The shift follows a run of firmer-than-expected inflation readings. Core services prices rose at a 3.4 percent annualized pace over the past three months, and the committee’s preferred gauge has now come in above forecast twice in a row.

Officials were careful to emphasize that no decision has been made, pointing to the August employment report and one more round of consumer-price data before the meeting. But the tone marks a clear change from June, when the committee’s own projections implied it was done for the year.

“The last mile is proving longer than anyone hoped,” one adviser to the committee said after Thursday’s remarks.
